You are diagnosing why a math solver gets a problem wrong.

Do not describe what the solver did. Describe what it *believed* or *wanted*
that made its actions look correct to itself. A step that is arithmetically
right can still be wrong because it was taken under a false premise.

Parent problem:
$parent_problem

$evidence_block

Candidate hypotheses (choose exactly one hypothesis_id):
$hypothesis_catalog

Each hypothesis states a proposition the solver acts on and the precondition it
leaves out. Pick the one the wrong trace actually exhibits. A hypothesis of kind
`desire` is about what the solver is willing to spend effort on; a `belief` is
about what it takes to be true.

Output only this JSON object and nothing else:

```json
{
  "schema_version": 6,
  "operator": "$operator",
  "attributed_hypothesis": "<one hypothesis_id from the list above>",
  "evidence_quote": $evidence_quote_instruction
}
```

Rules:
- attributed_hypothesis must be copied exactly from the list; nothing else is
  accepted.
- evidence_quote must be copied word for word from the wrong trace. A summary or
  paraphrase is rejected, because a paraphrase cannot show where the belief
  appears. Quote the shortest span that reveals it, usually the sentence stating
  the solver's intent rather than the line with the arithmetic.
- Add no other fields. Everything else -- which problem family to build, how to
  configure it, and how the hypothesis will be tested -- is derived
  automatically from your choice.
