casework: one visible problem has genuinely distinct cases requiring materially different treatments; uniform branches or seed-level variation do not qualify
induction: a size-n or recursive structure essentially depends on smaller instances; direct unrolling or closed-form substitution does not qualify
contradiction: the shortest solution has no direct route and must assume the negation of what is asked, then derive a structural impossibility -- a parity, divisibility, size, or well-ordering clash -- that rules out EVERY case at once. Checking finitely many candidates and reporting that the rest fail does not qualify, and neither does computing the answer directly and remarking afterwards that other values are impossible. Tie-breakers, in order: if the impossibility is exhibited by a quantity preserved through a process, choose invariant; if it is obtained by taking a minimal or maximal offender, choose extremal_principle; if the argument runs separately in each of several branches, choose casework.
invariant: a repeatable process is decided by a preserved or monotone quantity
extremal_principle: an extremal object or configuration is used structurally to force a sharp bound or conclusion; direct optimization of an explicit formula does not qualify
counting: a finite cardinality requires a nontrivial structural counting principle such as inclusion-exclusion, complement, bijection, product rule, pigeonhole, or double counting; small direct enumeration does not qualify
transformation: the shortest solution has no route in the setting as given and must first carry the problem into a DIFFERENT mathematical object -- another domain, structure, or ambient set -- solve it there, and read the answer back; the map is the content of the solution, not a step in it. Rearranging, substituting a variable, applying a standard formula, or optimizing an explicit expression all stay inside the original object and do not qualify. Tie-breakers, in order: if the move is a bijection used to count, choose counting; if what it produces is an explicit object certifying existence or sharpness, choose construction; if it exhibits a quantity preserved by a process, choose invariant; if your solution instead splits into branches, choose casework.
construction: an explicit construction certifies existence, attainability, sharpness, or a threshold; merely giving an example does not qualify
